Abstract

Agentic AI in regulated P&C insurance is constrained less by model capability than by the inability to produce durable decision evidence. Underwriting decisions persist for years, and regulators, courts, and customers routinely require carriers to demonstrate what was known at decision time, which rules applied, where discretion was exercised, and who held authority for exceptions. Current AI deployments optimize prediction and workflow speed but fail to preserve this decision state; debug traces and explainability artifacts satisfy engineers, not auditors. This paper defines a minimal decision-evidence construct and a decision-spine architecture that binds models, rules, human intervention, and escalation pathways into replayable, auditable records, illustrated through worked underwriting examples. Underwriting AI will scale in regulated insurance only when systems are designed to produce owned, defensible decisions that survive time.
